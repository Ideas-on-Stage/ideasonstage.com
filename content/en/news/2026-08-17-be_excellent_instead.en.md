There’s a quote I like by psychologist Harriet Braiker: “Striving for excellence motivates you; striving for perfection is demoralising.”

It’s a subtle but important distinction.

Perfection means chasing something impossible. Excellence means aiming high while staying grounded. You push yourself to do well, but you don’t expect to get everything right.

Nowhere is this more true than in public speaking.

Perfectionism leads to overthinking every tiny mistake. It adds stress and takes away from your ability to connect. But when you aim for excellence, you leave room for growth.

It’s better for your audience – and better for you.
